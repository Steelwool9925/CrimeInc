First Session, First Impression

Three player temperaments, two platforms, one question: does the first ten minutes of Underworld Empire earn a second visit?

2026‑09‑12 3 temperaments web + Android 6 sessions findings logged, not fixed

Method: each of three temperaments played a fresh guest account, with no prior knowledge, until the starting energy bar first ran dry — the natural stopping point a real first-time player would hit — extended in-session if a level-up refilled the bar, since that's a real thing the game does. Run once per platform, six sessions total.

Patient explorer — reads everything, checks every screen before acting. Impatient tapper — skips all text, taps the fastest path, never reads a toast. Skeptical min-maxer — works the numbers, looks for the efficient play, judges whether the pacing is fair.

What actually drives day-one return

Persona Session Read Open it again?
Patient explorer ~10 min Good writing, a real shop worth finding — undercut by a purchase that visibly did nothing until a hidden screen turned up. uncertain
Impatient tapper <15 sec Snappy and responsive, but the whole first session evaporated before anything could hook them. unlikely
Skeptical min-maxer ~8 min Found a genuine optimization — gear up, unlock a better job, level, refill, repeat — that rewarded analytical play. likely*

* on web. On Android, this exact persona's reward job went dead in their hands — see below.

The throughline: the moment-to-moment loop — tap job, resolve, toast, occasional level-up — is solid and never broke for anyone. Every problem sits in the layer around it: what the game shows, or doesn't, when a purchase happens, when a session ends, and whether what's on screen can be trusted at face value.

The one finding that matters most

Android · min-maxer session

The min-maxer did everything right: worked out the odds, saved for a weapon, bought it, equipped it, unlocked a better-paying job exactly as the numbers said they should — and then that job's own "Do job" button stopped responding to touch, permanently, for the rest of the session. Confirmed across more than five taps, different coordinates, different toast states, and a full navigation away and back. The job right next to it kept working the entire time.

Everything else in this batch is polish, feedback, or discoverability — friction that makes a first session feel worse but doesn't stop it. This one does. It takes the exact player most likely to stick around and lets the game go dead in their hands at the moment they're rewarded for playing well.

Web

Persona 1 — Patient explorer

Checked every screen before touching a job. Landed on a rocky start: the only legal job at level 1 failed three times running (a ~4% event on a stated 65% success rate — rare, but real) before the first payout landed. From there the session was smooth, and even turned up real depth.

Works wellThe Market is a genuine hidden gem

Every item has real, considered flavor text — "Second-hand from a police auction. Nobody asked why." But it isn't one of the five main nav tabs; it's a dashboard tile reachable only by scrolling past Crew, several screens down. A player who doesn't scroll that far never finds it.

FrictionBuying gear gives zero feedback

No auto-equip, no stat change, no "go equip this" nudge. The actual Equip control is a small, unlabeled icon button in the Market's own app bar that opens an "Inventory" overlay — genuinely easy to miss. Until found, a purchase looks exactly like money vanishing for nothing. Once found, the equip flow itself is clean — a clear "1 + 4" attack breakdown.

RetentionSession end is silent

At 0 energy, Home shows only a passive "+1 in 1m 00s" timer — no notification prompt, no "come back later," no suggested downtime activity. A player has to already know to go spend their cash at the Market; nothing points them there.

Works wellA real guest‑to‑account safety net

The Rap Sheet (profile) screen surfaces a "Create Account" upsell for converting the guest session — protects progress from a cleared browser, if a player happens to visit.

Persona 2 — Impatient tapper

Skipped the welcome text, skipped Home, went straight to the job button and mashed it.

Works wellRapid mashing does not drop inputs

Firing the same click three to four times faster than the toast could render still resolved every attempt correctly — energy and cash both landed exactly right when checked afterward. Snappy and trustworthy under abuse.

RetentionThe whole session is over in seconds

Ten energy, spent in two rapid bursts, zero exploration — well under fifteen seconds of input. Combined with the same silent session-end as persona 1, the net impression is "that was it?" rather than "I want more" — the opposite of what a first session should leave behind for someone who plays exactly this way.

Persona 3 — Skeptical min-maxer

Went in doing the math: at level 1 there's exactly one legal job — no real decision yet, a fair critique of the opening minutes. Computed cash and XP per energy, then made a deliberate play: save for Brass Knuckles (75 cash, +4 attack), since base 1 + 4 is exactly the 5 attack "Boost a Parked Car" needs — turning a locked, better-paying job legal before waiting on a level.

Works wellThe optimization pays off immediately

Confirmed: buying and equipping the weapon unlocked the job on the spot, at nearly 50% better cash-per-energy than the starter job. A satisfying, correct read of the system.

Works wellLeveling up fully refills energy

Confirmed directly: a level-up mid-grind took energy from 0 back to 10/10. A real, well-designed lever — efficient play is rewarded with more session, not capped by a hard wall.

FrictionA gating inconsistency

Once a job's level requirement is met, its "Do job" button renders enabled even if its separate stat requirement isn't. Tapping it wastes a round trip and returns a rejection banner — correctly refused before spending energy, so nothing is lost, but the button should grey out the same way it already does for unmet level or energy requirements.

Android

Same three personas, same method, on a local emulator with the server address baked in — matching how a real install would ship, no dev-only address screen at all. Automation here is direct touch input rather than browser tools, which incidentally clarified something: most of the web session's "did my click even register?" moments were a rendering lag, not dropped input. Tap, then wait a beat before judging the result, and Android was completely reliable for ordinary navigation. That reframes several web observations as one root cause — delayed repaint after a state change — rather than two.

First impressionHome can render fully blank right after account creation

Same underlying class as the web Properties bug: the account exists and the data is there, but the very first screen a new player ever sees can look frozen for a moment. Higher stakes than the web instance purely because of when it happens — first thing, first launch. Not reproduced on every run, so likely timing-dependent.

Works wellThe native layout is a step up

Six Home tiles — Jobs, Properties, Crew, Market, Territory, Rap Sheet — render as a tidy two-column grid instead of web's stretched, mostly-empty full-width cards. The job toasts and the level-up modal both look cleaner here too. If this game's current polish belongs to one platform, it's Android's.

BlockerThe reward job goes dead on touch

Detailed above — after unlocking "Boost a Parked Car" the correct way, its own button stopped responding for the rest of the session, while the job above it kept working. No error, no visual change, just silence at the exact moment of reward.

Lower confidenceRapid taps land less reliably than on web

Only around one in three rapid same-spot taps landed in a single burst, versus all of them on web. Flagged with less certainty — this may be an artifact of how the touch input was injected rather than a real difference from a human finger.
